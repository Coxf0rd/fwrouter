## Cache contract and residual bounds

The implementation bounds the completed process-local LRU at 256 entries, a 32 MiB aggregate admission-time estimate, and 24 MiB for one value. Admission sizing is capped at 50,000 visits/depth 64, with cycles and aliases deduplicated. Requested-key expiry is checked on each access; a sweep of at most 256 entries runs no more than once per second of cache activity. TTL starts after loader completion; TTL zero/negative results are not retained. Force refresh bypasses completed entries but coalesces with an existing same-key load. Errors are not cached. Exact connection-owner invalidation and event/holder fencing prevent stale publication into a replacement generation. These are source policy bounds, not a production-derived memory SLO or a strict process/RSS bound.

The remaining limits must stay visible:

- Inventory admission is incomplete: the evidence does not prove that every production owner/key/value shape stays within intended admission assumptions. Unknown, oversized, or unmeasurable graphs are returned but not retained; representative synthetic cases do not establish the production distribution. This is an explicit limitation for later resource attribution, not by itself a newly added Stage 5 entry criterion.
- Sizing is an admission-time estimate. Cached objects retain caller-owned mutable identity; mutation after admission can make the graph larger than its recorded estimate.
- Caller-held return values and in-flight loader/follower results are outside completed-cache byte accounting. Their total lifetime/concurrency envelope is not bounded by the entry and byte caps.
- Same-key followers retain the existing wait-deadline contract; this package did not add a finite queue/waiter bound. A slow/hung leader can therefore keep followers waiting under that existing contract.
- Adapter identity still uses the existing type/address key. Python object-address reuse after disposal is an acknowledged, unclosed identity limitation; no strong adapter registry was added.
- Expired values are reclaimed by later activity or owner clear. A fully idle cache may retain its already-bounded expired entries until then. The periodic sweep is opportunistic, not a background timer.
- Admission and hit-path accounting add CPU. Matched synthetic measurements showed higher cold-admission and warm-hit time; they support bounded retention, not a CPU-speedup claim.

No residual item here proves a production leak. Together they do prevent claiming a complete strict memory/follower bound. Keep the incomplete inventory/admission evidence visible in the existing owning register; do not represent synthetic distributions as production facts.
